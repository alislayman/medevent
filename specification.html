Scope agreement

Medical Event Staffing Platform

A two-sided marketplace connecting medical professionals — doctors, nurses, officials, cutmen, ambulance staff — with event organisers who need qualified on-site medical coverage.

01 — Overview

Overview

The platform connects medical professionals with event organisers who need medical staff on site — sports competitions, combat-sports events and public gatherings.

Status of this document. This revision incorporates every point raised in the client meeting of 29 July 2026 and the follow-up notes of 30 July 2026, and is intended to serve as the agreed scope of the project. Section 23 lists each item agreed, where it has been specified, and the phase it is assigned to.

Two user types

  • Professionals — build a verified profile, set specialties, trainings, availability and experience, then search and apply for events. They can also form teams with their peers and apply together.
  • Organisations — post events with requirements, attach a contract, and search and invite professionals. They can see which team a professional belongs to and its members.

Core loop

  • An organiser creates an event with requirements, coverage and materials; the event is either public or sent privately to support.
  • Professionals discover events and apply; organisers review the applicants and choose. Organisers can also discover professionals directly and invite them.
  • Professionals can form teams, invite peers and apply together as a team; organisers can view a professional's team and its members.
  • The two parties agree on remuneration — the professional may counter the proposed salary — then agree on a contract and the engagement proceeds.

Markets covered

  • The platform serves certified EU countries and the United States only. This coverage is stated publicly on the site (§6) and applied to the country lists used in profiles, licences and events.
  • A professional licensed in one country can still see and apply to events in another; where he holds no local licence he can request a paid accompaniment service (§13).

How it makes money

  • Monthly subscription tiers for professionals — Basic, Bronze, Silver, Gold.
  • Free signup for organisations, with payable premium services drawn from a catalogue (§13).
  • A paid verification badge for professionals.
  • VIP status for organisations and paid top placement for individual events (§17).

02 — Roles, taxonomy & use cases

Roles, taxonomy & use cases

Professional taxonomy — two layers

Professional roles are organised in two levels: a professional type and, beneath it, a subcategory. Both levels are reference data maintained by the admin team (§19, Entities → Professional sub-categories) rather than hard-coded.

  • Layer 1 — professional type: Médecin (doctor), Infirmier (nurse), Officiel (official), Cutman (combat-sports corner medic), Ambulancier (ambulance staff).
  • Layer 2 — subcategory: a further breakdown attached to each type, editable from the admin panel.

Profile fields per type

  • A common core of fields shared by every professional, whatever the type.
  • A set of fields specific to each professional type, shown only for that type.

Lead logic

Rule. Only a médecin can be a lead. A team must always have a lead, and that lead must be the médecin of the team — no other professional type may lead.
  • When an organiser creates an event, he indicates on the médecin role whether that role is required as a lead.
  • When a médecin applies to an event, he chooses whether he is applying as lead.
  • Within a team, the médecin is the lead; leadership can be transferred only under the rules in §10.

Conditions to sign (per account type)

  • Creating a profile requires the user to tick a configurable set of conditions.
  • The set is defined per account type — organisation, and per professional type.
  • The admin panel has a dedicated section to add, edit, remove, disable and enable these conditions (§19).
  • The submit action stays disabled until every condition is checked.
  • Acceptance is recorded per user with a timestamp and the version of the condition accepted — this is what makes the contact-sharing rule in §14 enforceable.

Licences, countries & accompaniment

  • A doctor holds a licence per country, recorded on his profile.
  • He can nevertheless see and apply to foreign events — visibility is not restricted by licence.
  • Where he holds no licence in the event's country, he can apply and request accompaniment: a service the platform provides to support him in practising there.
  • The accompaniment request is handled as a quotation (devis), appears in the service catalogue, and is visible to the admin team.

Organisation

  • Event organisers — sports events, competitions and public events. Verified at the final stage of profile completion (§8), and optionally flagged VIP (§17).

Admin / Support (internal)

  • Maintains reference entities, verifies credentials, manages badges, handles service requests and quotations, messages users, moderates content and resolves disputes. Detailed in §19.

03 — Technical stack

Technical stack

The backend language is fixed (Python). The rest is chosen for a robust, GDPR-appropriate build that also serves a future mobile app from one codebase.

  • Backend

    Django + Django REST Framework

    One clean, versioned API serving both the web app and the future mobile app, with a dedicated back-office for entities, verification, support and quotations.

  • Frontend · web

    Next.js (React) + TypeScript

    Tailwind CSS and shadcn/ui for a responsive, accessible interface; server-side rendering for fast, SEO-friendly public pages.

  • Mobile

    Responsive → PWA → native

    Responsive web first, then an installable PWA with push, then a native iOS + Android app via React Native / Expo.

  • Database

    PostgreSQL

    Relational core with JSONB for flexible per-type profile fields and full-text search to power matching and filtering.

  • Auth & anti-bot

    JWT + reCAPTCHA

    Django auth with JWT for the API; reCAPTCHA on sign in, sign up, password reset and password change.

  • Payments

    Stripe · PayPal

    Stripe (cards + tiers) first and PayPal later, with webhooks keeping subscription state in sync.

  • Email & SMS

    Brevo

    Decided. One French provider for both channels — account confirmation, password reset and notification email, plus the one-time codes that verify a phone number. Marketing campaigns later use the same account.

  • File storage

    Scaleway Object Storage (S3)

    S3-compatible object storage in France for avatars, event banners and credential documents, encrypted, with server-side resizing. Same private network as the application.

  • AI

    AI search & support chatbot

    AI-assisted matching and ranking in search, plus a support chatbot layered on the same API in a later phase.

  • Hosting

    Scaleway · France (fr-par)

    Decided. Application containers, managed PostgreSQL and object storage in one private network, with the database reachable only privately. Separate projects isolate test from production.

  • Source & CI/CD

    GitHub + GitHub Actions

    Automated lint → test → build → deploy; mobile builds via Expo EAS.

Billing integrity. PlanGrant is deliberately a separate table from Subscription. 07 — Professional profile

Professional profile

Identity & classification

  • Profile picture.
  • Professional type (layer 1): Médecin, Infirmier, Officiel, Cutman, Ambulancier.
  • Subcategory (layer 2): selected from the subcategories attached to the chosen type; the list is maintained in the admin panel.
  • Common core fields shared by every professional, plus fields specific to the selected type, rendered only for that type.
  • Medical specialty: one or several.
  • Diploma: year, place, university, country.

Complementary trainings (treated like diplomas)

  • Systematic (predefined) options:
    • Traumatologie du sport
    • Gestion d’urgence terrain
    • ATLS
    • PHTLS
  • Free-text field for other trainings.
  • Document upload (included in the verification).

Registration with the medical board (tableau de l’ordre)

  • Département / region
  • Date
  • Document
  • Country(ies) where the licence is valid

Licences abroad

  • Licences are held per country. Holding none in a given country does not hide that country's events.
  • From an event in a country he is not licensed in, the professional can apply and tick “request accompaniment”, which raises a quotation handled by the admin team (§13).

Experience & other fields

  • Event volume / experience captured as ranges (0–5, 5–10, …) — surfaced in search results through the detail hint (§11).
  • Languages.
  • Certificates → document uploads.
  • Availability: calendar or date ranges so organisers can filter by who is free.
  • Preferred language, editable at any time (§16).

Conditions to sign

  • The conditions configured for that professional type must all be ticked; submit stays disabled until they are.
  • One of these conditions is the undertaking not to share personal contact details, which the messaging rules in §14 enforce.

Profile completion

  • The Complete profile action opens a stepper: completed steps are marked green and a completeness percentage is shown throughout.
  • The fields in the stepper are the same fields as the profile — one source of truth. The user can fill them from the stepper or directly from the profile, in either order.
  • Access gating applies until the profile is complete (§11).

Professional agenda

  • The professional's agenda is reachable from the top right of the interface, or from a dedicated menu entry — placement to be settled in design.
  • It shows confirmed engagements, upcoming events applied to, and declared availability.

08 — Organisation profile & verification

Organisation profile & verification

  • Organisation details: name, type, country, logo / profile picture.
  • Requirements: specialties / roles typically needed, event types, typical locations.
  • Contact and billing information.
  • Conditions to sign for the organisation account type; submit disabled until all are ticked.
  • Preferred language, editable at any time.

Profile completion

  • Same mechanism as the professional: a stepper with green completed steps and a completeness percentage, reading and writing the same fields as the profile itself.
  • Until the profile is complete, the organisation can open the search pages but sees no results (§11).

Verification — final step of completion

Verification is the last stage of completing an organisation profile, and can be satisfied in one of two ways.

  • 1 · Domain verification. The organisation supplies a business email on its own domain. A message is sent to that address; once the organisation confirms it, the account is considered verified — no human review needed.
  • 2 · Requested validation. Where there is no business email, the organisation requests a manual validation reviewed by the admin team. This service is free for now but is carried in the service catalogue with an amount of €0 so it can be priced later without a code change (§13).

VIP status

  • An organisation can be flagged VIP by the admin team, through payment, or on request — see §17.
The organisation field list is indicative and should be expanded — the full list, formats and which fields are mandatory still need to be defined, since they drive the completeness percentage.

09 — Events, coverage & contracts

Events, coverage & contracts

Event creation (by organiser)

  • Details: title, date(s), location, budget or range.
  • Sport / discipline — football, basketball, MMA and so on. Held as a reference entity in the admin panel, shown on the event and usable as a search filter (§11).
  • Roles and headcount, role by role — for example 2 doctors, 3 nurses, 1 ambulance crew — with a total number of professionals derived from the breakdown and shown on the event.
  • Lead requirement: on the médecin role, the organiser indicates whether that role is required as a lead.
  • Imagery: a banner image and a circular profile image. If no banner is supplied, the banner section is not rendered at all.

Coverage offered by the organiser

The organiser specifies what the engagement covers, so professionals can judge an offer at a glance:

  • Accommodation (logement)
  • Food (nourriture)
  • Transport
  • Remuneration (rémunération) — the proposed amount
  • Further coverage items can be added as reference data

Materials provided (matériels mis à disposition)

  • The event lists the materials the organiser makes available on site.
  • Materials are a reference entity maintained in the admin panel (Entities → Materials), so the list stays consistent and filterable.

Salary negotiation

Rule. A professional on a Gold subscription may propose a salary different from the one advertised. The organiser can then refuse and keep the initial salary, accept the professional's amount, or counter with an amount in between the two.
  • Example: the event advertises 300; a Gold professional applies asking 350; the organiser accepts 350, holds at 300, or counters at, say, 325.
  • Professionals below Gold apply at the advertised amount; the counter-offer control is replaced by an upgrade prompt.
  • Every proposal is stored with its author and outcome, giving a full negotiation history on the application.
  • Negotiation is reflected in the application lifecycle (§5).

Contract

  • A contract is auto-generated by the system and attached to the event.
  • The organiser can edit or replace specific lines, or attach their own contract instead.
  • The agreed remuneration flows from the negotiation into the contract.

Visibility

  • Public — listed and searchable by professionals.
  • Direct to support — sent privately to support for handling.
  • VIP and promoted events are surfaced first in results (§17).

Application / invitation

  • Professionals apply to public events individually or as a team; a médecin states whether he applies as lead.
  • The organiser chooses among the applicants — applying does not confer a place.
  • Where a professional cannot apply — role already filled, not eligible, subscription tier too low on a VIP event — the event is still shown, marked “not available” with the reason, rather than hidden.
  • Organisers invite professionals and can see which team a professional belongs to and its members (§10).

10 — Teams

Teams

Teams are a professional feature — they are not owned by organisations. Professionals group together, invite each other and can apply to events as a single team. A team has a profile picture of its own.

Lead

Rule. A lead is required for every team, and the lead must be a médecin. No other professional type can lead a team.
  • Transfer of leadership: only the current lead can initiate it. He nominates another médecin in the team, and that nominee must accept. On acceptance the previous lead loses leadership — there is never more than one lead.
  • A team cannot be left without a lead: if the only médecin leaves, the team is flagged and cannot apply until a lead is in place.

Membership & permissions

  • Create a team and become its owner; invite peers to join.
  • When inviting a member, the inviter sets the permission “Can apply to events” — whether that member may submit applications on behalf of the team.
  • When a member accepts an invitation, he chooses whether he will accept applications by default — that is, whether team applications involving him need his explicit confirmation each time or are taken as accepted.
  • A professional can belong to several teams at once (multi-team membership).
  • Apply to public events as a team, in addition to applying individually.

Organisations

  • Can see which team a professional belongs to and view the team's members and its lead.
  • Can invite a professional and, through them, reach the team — but do not create or manage teams.

11 — Search, matching & gating

Search, matching & access gating

Access gating on profile completeness

Rule. A user cannot search until his profile is complete. The pages remain reachable — they open normally but return no results, with a message inviting the user to complete his profile in order to search. The same applies to viewing other users' profiles: the page opens, the content stays hidden.
  • This keeps the interface discoverable rather than blocking navigation outright, and gives the completeness percentage (§7) a concrete purpose.
  • The empty state links straight into the profile stepper.

Organiser → professionals

  • Filters: type and subcategory, specialty, certifications, languages, availability, verification status, location, licence country.
  • Each result shows, on the card itself:
    • the flags of the countries where the professional is licensed to practise, so an organiser can see at a glance whether he may work their event;
    • the languages he speaks.
  • Each result carries a hint icon: hovering or tapping it reveals more detail about the professional — experience and event volume, trainings, languages and other profile highlights — without leaving the results list.
  • The team a professional belongs to is visible from the result.

Professional → events

  • Filters: role, specialty, location, date, sport (football, basketball, MMA, …), coverage offered, materials provided.
  • Remuneration filter — filtering by pay is reserved for Silver and Gold subscribers. Further tier-gated filters are to be confirmed by the client.
  • The sport is shown on every event card alongside the role breakdown.
  • Only upcoming events are shown — past events never appear in search.
  • Each result shows the headcount by role (x doctors, y nurses, …) and the total number of professionals sought.
  • Result ordering reflects the professional's own relationship to each event, in this order:
    • Accepted
    • Pending
    • Other events
    • Declined — always last
  • Each result displays that status explicitly, and events he cannot apply to are marked “not available” rather than removed.
  • VIP organisations' events and promoted events appear above the rest (§17).

Map view

  • Events can be browsed on a map as well as in a list, with the two views sharing the same filter set — including sport and the tier-gated remuneration filter.
  • VIP and promoted events are distinguished on the map as they are in the list (§17).

Matching

  • Filter-based to start; AI-assisted matching, ranking and recommendations in a later phase, alongside saved searches and alerts.

12 — Subscriptions & payments

Subscriptions, payments & verification badge

Professional tiers (monthly)

  • Basic (free) + Bronze + Silver + Gold.
  • What each tier unlocks is largely still to be defined — see §22. Three entitlements are already fixed:
    • Gold — applying to VIP events (§17).
    • Gold — proposing a salary other than the advertised one (§9).
    • Silver and Gold — filtering event search by remuneration (§11).
  • Entitlements are held as data against each tier, not written into the code, so the plan definitions can be settled and revised without a release.

Organisation

  • Free signup; organisations pay for premium services (§13) and for VIP or promoted placement (§17) rather than a subscription.

Payment methods

  • Stripe (cards) first; PayPal added in a later phase.

Manually granted plans

  • An admin can place a user on a specific plan for free, or against an amount paid in cash.
  • When granting the plan the admin records the amount actually received, which may be 0.
  • Because plan prices change over time, these grants are stored in a separate table with the amount captured at the moment of the grant.
  • Grants do not feed the dashboards: the analytics always report the real number of subscriptions taken and the real amount collected through the platform.

Verification badge

  • Separate payment; unlocks the verified badge after document review by support.
  • The badge feature can be enabled or disabled globally from the admin panel. Disabling it stops new badges being issued but does not affect users who already hold one.

Later phases

  • Yearly payment with an annual offer, referral programme (parrainage), voucher codes and user discounts — see §20.

14 — Communication

Communication

Messaging

  • In-app messaging between all users: professional ↔ organiser, professional ↔ professional, and with the support team.
  • Direct messaging first; a support chatbot is added in a later phase to handle common questions and triage before a human steps in.

Contact-detail protection

Rule. Sending phone numbers and email addresses through the messaging system is blocked. The sender is shown an alert warning that the account may be blocked, recalling that he signed a condition not to share his contact details.
  • Detection covers plain and obfuscated forms of phone numbers and email addresses.
  • Any contact information is hidden in message threads, whatever its origin.
  • The warning refers back to the specific condition the user accepted at profile creation (§2), which makes it enforceable.
  • Repeat attempts are surfaced to the admin team for moderation.

Contact support

  • Both professionals and organisations have a contact support action — presented clearly and made easy to reach in the interface, not buried in a menu.
  • The request requires a motif (reason), chosen from a list maintained as a reference entity in the admin panel.
  • Requests land in the admin messages queue with their motif for filtering.

Messages from the admin team

  • The admin team can message any user, either through the in-app messaging system or by email (§19).

AI features

  • AI is applied especially in search — assisting matching, ranking and recommendations — and powers the later support chatbot.

Notifications

  • In-app + email notifications for applications, invitations, salary proposals, team and leadership requests, messages, contract / event updates and verification status.

15 — Verification & security

Account verification & security

Account verification

  • Email verification at signup.
  • Mobile (SMS) verification.
  • Organisation verification by business domain or requested validation (§8).
  • Credential verification is handled separately via document review (§7, §12).

Verification page

  • The verification page is enriched with a proper title and a description explaining what is being verified, what documents are expected and what happens next.

Verification badge control

  • A setting in the admin panel enables or disables the verification badge across the platform.
  • Turning it off does not affect users who already hold the badge — they keep it.

Bot protection

  • reCAPTCHA (or an equivalent “I am not a robot” challenge) is applied to:
    • Sign in
    • Sign up
    • Reset password
    • Change password

16 — Languages & markets

Languages & markets

Interface languages

  • Full user-interface translation in French and English.
  • Bilingual content / data fields where relevant.

Preferred language

  • A preferred language is captured at signup.
  • The default is English, except where the user is in a French-speaking country — France, Canada, several African countries — in which case French is pre-selected.
  • The user can change it at any time from his profile.
  • The site opens in the user's preferred language on every subsequent visit.

Managing the texts Phase 2

  • Every text used across the site is editable from the admin panel, in every language including English.
  • The client can add a language himself and translate into it; anything not yet translated falls back so nothing ever renders blank.
  • Each text shows where on the site it appears, so it can be found without hunting.
  • Changes are live immediately — no publication step and no deployment.
  • Right-to-left languages such as Arabic mirror the whole interface; that is Phase 5 (§20).

Markets

  • Coverage is limited to certified EU countries and the United States, stated publicly (§6) and applied consistently to country lists.

17 — Visibility & promotion (VIP)

Visibility & promotion

Two mechanisms raise an organisation or an event above ordinary results — a standing VIP status, and paid placement bought per event.

VIP moved to Phase 2 and is already built (8 Aug 2026): the status on organisations and events, the Gold-only restriction on applying, the VIP-first ordering and the gold-on-black treatment are all delivered, at no additional cost. Promoted events remain in Phase 4.

VIP organisations

  • An organisation can be marked VIP in three ways: set by the admin team, through payment, or on request (a catalogue service, organisations only — §13).
  • Their events appear at the top of professionals' search results.
  • Applying to a VIP organisation's events is restricted to Gold professionals; other tiers see the event marked “not available” with the reason.

VIP events

  • An individual event can also carry VIP status; organisations can request it for a given event.
  • The same Gold-only restriction on applications applies.

Visual treatment

  • VIP events are styled gold on a black background, in list, map and detail views, so a marquee event — a UFC card, an international championship — reads as premium at a glance.
  • The treatment is derived from the tier palette variables rather than hard-coded, so it stays consistent with the Basic / Bronze / Silver / Gold styling used elsewhere.

Promoted events

  • An organisation can pay for top placement, event by event, independently of VIP status.
  • Ordering precedence in results: VIP and promoted events first, then the professional's own relationship to each event (accepted → pending → others → declined) as described in §11.
Pricing for VIP status and for promoted placement is still to be set — see §22.

19 — Admin panel

Admin / back-office panel

A private back-office for the internal admin / support team. The early phases ship the essentials needed to run the marketplace; more advanced tooling is added in later phases.

Entities — reference data

A dedicated Entities menu item, with one sub-menu entry per entity, holds the reference data the rest of the platform reads. Each entity supports add, edit, remove, disable and enable.

  • Professional sub-categories — the second layer of the role taxonomy (§2).
  • Conditions to sign, per profile type — the checkboxes a user must tick to create a profile; versioned so acceptances stay traceable.
  • Materials — the equipment an organiser can declare as provided on an event (§9).
  • Sports — the disciplines an event can be attached to and filtered by (§9, §11).
  • Support motifs — the reasons available on a contact-support request (§14).
  • Service catalogue — payable services, their audience and amounts, including €0 items (§13).
  • Further entities to be added as they are identified.

Messages

  • A dedicated Messages menu item with filters — by user, type, motif, status and date.
  • The admin can send a message to any user, either through the in-app messaging system or by email.
  • Contact-sharing violations flagged by the messaging rules (§14) surface here for moderation.

Users, profiles & verification

  • User & profile management: view and search all professionals and organisations; approve, suspend or ban accounts; edit or correct profile data.
  • Credential verification: review uploaded documents (diplomas, certificates, ordre registration, insurance) and approve or reject them; grant or revoke the verification badge.
  • Verification badge switch: enable or disable the badge platform-wide, without affecting users who already hold it.
  • Organisation verification: handle requested validations where no business domain is available.

Subscriptions, plans & payments

  • See who is on which tier and their payment status; handle verification-badge payments and basic refunds.
  • Grant a plan manually — free, or against a cash amount recorded at the time of the grant (possibly €0), stored in a separate table and excluded from the analytics so reported revenue stays real (§12).

Services, quotations & VIP

  • Receive requests for every catalogue service — private search, team composition, VIP, cross-border accompaniment, organisation validation — and build, send and track quotations (sent, accepted, invoiced, paid).
  • Grant or revoke VIP status for organisations and events; manage paid top placement (§17).

Events, contracts & moderation

  • View all events (public and sent-to-support); handle direct-to-support events; access contracts.
  • Basic moderation of profiles, events and messages.

Content & translations

  • Manage static pages (Home, About, Licensing) and FR / EN translations.

Billing dashboard & analytics Phase 2 · delivered

  • A monthly billing dashboard reporting:
    • Number of users per type, and the total
    • Revenue per plan, and the total
  • Figures reflect real subscriptions and real amounts collected; manually granted plans are excluded.
  • Wider analytics: signups, active users per role, verification throughput, events posted, application / match rates.

Advanced — later phases extra

  • Review & evaluation management: enable or disable evaluations globally or per user; keep reviews private; send private feedback to help a party improve.
  • Chatbot & AI management: configure the support chatbot and tune AI-assisted search.
  • Roles & permissions Phase 2 — scoped admin and support sub-roles. Today a single role covers both, so anyone with support access holds full admin rights. The audit log itself is already built and append-only at both application and database level; the audit tooling beyond it is not planned.
  • Advanced moderation & troubleshooting: reports/flags queue, broadcast announcements, and viewing an account “as the user”.
  • Growth tooling: yearly payment and annual offers, referral programme (parrainage), voucher codes and user discounts.

22 — Assumptions & questions

Assumptions, exclusions & open questions

Assumptions

  • Prices are indicative development allocations for an initial build; final quotes depend on confirmed scope, and each phase is delivered and invoiced in sequence.
  • All prices are development only and exclude hosting, domain and every third-party subscription / usage fee (estimated in §21): payment transaction fees, SMS gateway, object storage, email service, AI / LLM usage, maps / other APIs and app-store fees.
  • The Phase 1 + Phase 2 total was held at €2,630 through the scope agreed on 29–30 July 2026. The revision of 8 August 2026 adds localization (€500) and admin permissions (€100), taking the core build to €3,230. VIP and the analytics dashboards moved into Phase 2 at no cost because the work was already complete.

Exclusions

  • Phase 3 — hold & release on the engagement fee.
  • Phase 4 — reviews, participation history, advanced matching, AI features, support chatbot, push notifications, PayPal, promoted events, the growth tooling (yearly billing, referrals, vouchers, discounts) and marketing campaigns.
  • Phase 5 — the native mobile application and right-to-left language support.

Open questions carried from the meeting of 29/07/2026

  • Define the plans. Confirm the four tiers (Basic free + Bronze / Silver / Gold) and define exactly what each one unlocks. Three entitlements are fixed — Gold applies to VIP events, Gold counter-offers on salary, Silver and Gold filter by remuneration — everything else is open, and it is the single largest blocker on development.
  • Further tier-gated filters. Beyond remuneration, which additional search filters should be reserved for Silver and Gold — the client indicated he would review this in detail.
  • Sport taxonomy. The initial list of sports, whether it is flat or grouped (combat sports → MMA, boxing), and whether an event may carry more than one.
  • Subcategory common logic. How the second taxonomy layer behaves across professional types — whether subcategories are shared or strictly per type, and which profile fields they drive.
  • SMS and email providers. To be selected and the details sent to Soufiane.

Other open questions to confirm

  • VIP & promoted events: the price of VIP status, the price of top placement per event, and how long each lasts.
  • Cross-border accompaniment: what the service actually delivers, its price and the turnaround the platform commits to.
  • Certified countries: confirm the exact list of certified EU countries alongside the US, and whether coverage restricts event creation as well as display.
  • Profile completeness: which fields count towards the percentage, and what threshold counts as “complete” for unlocking search.
  • Verification badge: one-time or recurring payment? Who verifies credentials, and against which authority (ordre des médecins)?
  • Auto-generated contracts: who supplies the base template, and legal review is needed (medical-staffing liability). How the negotiated salary is reflected in the contract.
  • Teams: who can remove a member, and what happens to a team application if a member leaves? Can an organiser invite a whole team, or only an individual who then brings the team?
  • Team applications: how is payment / contracting handled for a team engagement — one contract for the team, or one per member?
  • Contact blocking: what counts as a violation, and what the consequence actually is — warning, temporary suspension or block.
  • AI features: which model / provider (EU-hosted for GDPR?), what data may be sent to it, and what is the acceptable running-cost ceiling?
  • Support chatbot: scope for the first release — FAQ / triage only, or acting on the account? Escalation path to a human.
  • Organisation profile: finalise the full field list, formats and which fields are mandatory.
  • Reviews & ratings: mutual (both sides) or one-way? Public or private by default?
  • GDPR: sensitive medical & credential data — EU / France hosting, consent management, data export / erasure, document encryption and audit logs.
  • Payment currency and VAT handling across the EU and the US.
